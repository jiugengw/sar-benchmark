==============================================================================
6. WHAT THIS SUGGESTS (hints, not verdicts: confirm with an ablation)
==============================================================================
- 0.68% of pixels are saturated at 255: the bright tail is already clipped; percentile-clipping the top adds little, gamma/log mainly helps the dark/mid range.
- Noise std scales with local mean (exponent 0.92): multiplicative speckle in a nearly linear scale. A log transform (makes speckle additive) is a principled option to test.
- Speckle is spatially correlated (lag-1 > 0.3). Despeckling filters that assume i.i.d. speckle (Lee, Kuan) are less effective; compare a 256x256 group vs a 512x512 group: a much higher value in one suggests that group was upsampled.
- Lag correlation differs a lot between image-size groups: the groups have different resolution/processing history; consider whether stretching 256->512 hides that from the model.
- 50% of boxes are under 16 px at 512: keep any filter window <=3x3 (or none); a 7x7 filter is as large as the object.
- 45% of boxes are barely brighter than their surroundings: smoothing will hurt these; compare TCR/ratio before and after any filter and reject filters that lower them.
- 5.4% of boxes have no anchor with IoU>=0.5 (default ratios): try --anchor-profile wide.
- Class and image size are strongly linked (Cramer V 0.54): the model can read class from resampling blur even after stretching to 512. Treat classification gains with suspicion; consider a size-aware holdout.
- Brightness alone explains 43% of between-class variance: global intensity transforms change a class cue, not just noise.
- Duplicates / near-duplicates exist inside train: a random 90/10 split puts copies on both sides and inflates local scores; group duplicates before splitting.
- Normalisation: train mean/std at native res = 0.2657/0.2056 vs 0.2526/0.2133 in data.py. Recompute after ANY intensity transform and apply it identically in make_submission.py.
